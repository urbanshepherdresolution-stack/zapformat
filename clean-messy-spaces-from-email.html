Why Email Formatting Produces Chaotic Spacing & Line Wraps

Copying content from modern email clients—including Microsoft Outlook, Apple Mail, Gmail, and Thunderbird—often turns clean correspondence into unreadable text when pasted into word processors, databases, or CRM tools. This layout distortion occurs because email transmission protocols are governed by legacy standards originally designed for terminal displays.

Under the historical RFC 2822 and RFC 5322 Internet Message Format specifications, outgoing mail transfer agents (MTAs) frequently enforce hard line limits (traditionally 78 characters per line). When an email traverses mail servers, the system automatically injects hard carriage returns (\r\n) into the body text. Furthermore, Rich Text (RTF) and HTML email formats inject non-breaking space entities (&nbsp;) to preserve visual alignment, creating random double spacing and uneven indentation when copied as raw text.

Nested Quoting and Forwarding Artifacts

Forwarded conversations and multi-recipient replies accumulate compounding formatting artifacts:

  • Nested Quote Carets: Each iteration of a reply thread appends leading angle brackets (>, >>, >>>) along with arbitrary space padding.
  • Outlook Space Preservation: Microsoft Outlook's Word-based rendering engine inserts proprietary paragraph styles and hidden MSO tags that split words across lines.
  • Interleaved Tab Characters: Copying formatted email tables converts column gaps into irregular tab clusters and ragged spacing blocks.
