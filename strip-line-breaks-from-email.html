Why Email Messages Suffer from Artificial Line Breaks

Copying text from an email message into a document editor, CMS, or database often turns smooth writing into a jagged column of short lines. Even though the text appeared normal when viewed in an email application like Gmail, Microsoft Outlook, or Apple Mail, pasting it reveals that sentences are chopped every few words. This behavior is a direct result of historical email transmission standards.

The core Internet Message Format specifications—governed by RFC 2822 and RFC 5322—mandate that lines of plain text in email messages should not exceed 78 characters (with a hard maximum of 998 characters). To ensure reliable transit across older mail transfer agents (MTAs), email software automatically injects hard carriage return and line feed sequences (\r\n) at fixed visual widths:

  • Fixed-Column Line Folding: Mail user agents automatically fold long lines into chunks under 80 characters. When copied to another application, these carriage returns are treated as true paragraph breaks, preventing sentences from wrapping naturally.
  • Compounding Reply Carets: Quoting in email threads introduces angle brackets (>, >>) at the start of each line. As threads progress through replies and forwards, these brackets lock hard-wrapped lines into place and fragment sentences further.
  • Non-Breaking Spaces (&nbsp;): HTML emails frequently pair hard wraps with non-breaking spaces to preserve visual padding, creating irregular gaps when pasted into code or plain-text editors.
  • Uneven Paragraph Gaps: Converting formatted emails back and forth creates an erratic mix of single line breaks within sentences and three or four empty lines between paragraphs.
